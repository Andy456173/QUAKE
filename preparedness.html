01 / BEFORE AN EARTHQUAKE

Four small steps.
A stronger start.

Start with these four essentials. Open a card when you want the full guidance.

01

Secure your space.

Reduce the things that can fall, break or block your exit.

FURNITUREEXITS
See what to check
  • Move heavy objects to lower shelves and away from beds, sofas and desks.
  • Check bookcases, televisions, mirrors, hanging lights and large appliances.
  • Ask an adult or qualified installer to secure tall furniture with suitable wall fixings.
  • Keep corridors, stairs and exits clear. Know where sturdy tables and desks are.
  • Store household chemicals safely. Have competent adults check LPG and heavy appliances.

Suspect structural damage? Ask a qualified engineer. Do not attempt structural or utility repairs yourself.

02

Make a family plan.

Everyone should know where to go and how to reconnect.

MEETING PLACECONTACTS
Build your plan
  • Choose a nearby safe meeting spot and a second one outside your neighbourhood.
  • Pick a trusted contact outside your area. Write their number on a paper card.
  • Plan accessible routes, a backup route and support for older people, children and pets.
  • Ask schools about attendance, family contact and authorised pickup arrangements.
  • Practise a short message: “I'm safe. I'm at ___. I'm with ___.”

Keep a paper route sketch and contact card with your supplies, without exposing personal details publicly.

03

Gather essentials.

Keep useful supplies nearby, not necessarily expensive supplies.

WATERMEDICINE
What matters most?
  • Prioritise safe water, food, torches, batteries, a power bank and a first-aid kit.
  • Include prescribed medicines, medical lists and individual accessibility needs.
  • Keep sturdy closed shoes and a torch beside the bed.
  • Check expiry dates and recharge equipment regularly.

For a printable-style supply list, explore the three kit options below.

Compare emergency kits →
04

Learn & practise.

A calm drill helps your household remember what to do.

DRILLSFIRST AID
Try a safe practice
  1. Explain the drill beforehand and choose a clear, safe space.
  2. Practise Drop, Cover and Hold On, adapting for different mobility needs.
  3. After the imaginary shaking, discuss exits, meeting places and who needs help.
  4. Learn first aid and CPR with a recognised instructor.
  5. Review and repeat the plan when household circumstances change.

Never shake furniture, create smoke, block exits or surprise people with frightening drills.

✦

Focus on one room first. The place where you sleep or study is a useful place to begin.

02 / BUILD YOUR SUPPLIES

Your kit. Your needs.

Tap a kit to see what belongs inside. Begin with supplies you already have and build up over time.

GRAB AND GO

A portable go-kit.

Prepare essentials for leaving home. A common planning target is at least 3 days of supplies, adjusted to your family's needs and what can safely be carried.

03DAYS

What to include

  • Drinking water and ready-to-eat food
  • Necessary medicines and a medication list
  • Torch, whistle, batteries and power bank
  • First-aid, hygiene and spare clothing
  • Copies of key documents and some cash
STAY READY AT HOME

A larger home reserve.

Where practical, work toward 2 weeks of essentials in case water, power, shops or transport are disrupted. The supplies do not all need to fit in one bag.

14DAYS

What to include

  • Safe drinking water and long-lasting food
  • Manual can opener and eating utensils
  • Radio, lighting and spare batteries
  • Sanitation and hygiene supplies
  • Infant, disability and pet essentials
NIGHT-TIME READINESS

Keep a small bed kit.

Store these items next to or safely attached near your bed so they stay within reach even when furniture shifts.

24/ 7

What to include

  • Sturdy, closed-toe shoes
  • Torch and whistle
  • Glasses or daily mobility aids
  • A suitable dust mask
  • Essential personal items
Additional kit advice: medical needs, storage and expiry dates

Consider hearing-aid batteries, mobility-device charging, glasses and other communication aids. Ask your clinician or pharmacist about safe medicine reserves and storage needs. For medical equipment, plan backup power with the provider or care team.

Label a kit review date. Replace expired items safely, charge power banks, test torches and store water in clean, suitable containers. Keep sensitive documents protected.

03 / IF SHAKING BEGINS

DROP. COVER.
HOLD ON.

Protect yourself right away. In most indoor situations, stay inside and use nearby cover. Don't run outside during the shaking.

01

DROP

Get onto your hands and knees so you are less likely to fall.

02

COVER

Get beneath a sturdy nearby desk or table and shield your head and neck.

03

HOLD ON

Hold the shelter and stay protected until the shaking stops.

NO TABLE NEARBY?Stay low, away from windows and falling objects, and protect your head and neck with your arms.
04 / DIFFERENT PLACES, DIFFERENT ACTIONS

Where are you right now?

Choose a situation to see the most relevant steps. These are general guidelines; always follow local emergency instructions.

INDOORS

Stay inside. Find cover.

Move only a short distance away from glass or unstable objects. Drop under a sturdy nearby desk or table. Do not use lifts, rush outside or stand in a doorway. In a classroom, follow the teacher's practised routine.

IN BED

Stay in bed. Protect your head.

Cover your head and neck with a pillow and remain there until shaking stops. Avoid stepping onto possible broken glass. Put on sturdy shoes before walking afterward.

OUTSIDE OR IN A CROWD

Stay out of falling-object zones.

Outside, move to a nearby clear space away from buildings, trees and power lines; get low and shield your head. In a crowded indoor venue, do not rush for an exit. Protect yourself in place until shaking stops.

IN A VEHICLE

Pull over safely.

The driver should stop away from bridges, overhead wires and other hazards. Set the parking brake, keep seatbelts fastened and stay inside until shaking stops. If a power line lands on the vehicle, remain inside and wait for trained help.

NEAR THE COAST

Be aware of tsunami danger.

Protect yourself while the ground shakes. After strong or prolonged coastal shaking, promptly move inland or to higher ground along the local tsunami evacuation route. Do not wait to see a wave; follow official warnings.

05 / INCLUSIVE PREPAREDNESS

Safety should work
for everyone.

The safest movement may be different for each person. Do not force a position that could cause a fall. Discuss and practise suitable steps in advance.

Using a canePractical alternatives+

If possible, Drop, Cover and Hold On. Otherwise, sit safely on a nearby chair or bed and shield your head and neck with both hands. Keep your cane nearby.

Walker or wheelchairLock, cover and hold+

Lock wheels if applicable. Remain seated in a wheelchair, or sit on a walker's seat when available. Shield your head and neck with your arms, a book or pillow and hold on. Do not attempt a risky transfer to the floor.

Agree on how support people can help without pulling someone from their equipment.

Hearing, vision or communication needsKeep support accessible+

Keep glasses, hearing aids, batteries, mobility devices and communication aids within reach. Agree on clear visual, written or touch-based alerts that suit individual preferences. Plan accessible routes, backup assistance and equipment charging.

Always ask what assistance a person wants instead of assuming.

06 / AFTER THE SHAKING

The shaking stops.
Safety continues.

Take these actions in order, as conditions allow. Expect aftershocks and changing hazards.

01

Check people.

Look for injuries, wear sturdy shoes near debris and provide first aid only within your training.

02

Check the building.

Leave damaged spaces carefully when safe. Use stairs, avoid unstable walls and do not re-enter until cleared.

03

Watch for hazards.

Stay away from fallen wires. If you smell gas, leave; avoid flames and switches. Let trained people handle utilities.

04

Reconnect.

Send a brief message, use your meeting plan, preserve battery power and follow reliable official updates.

!
Another shake?

Drop, Cover and Hold On again. Aftershocks can damage already weakened structures.

!
If trapped

Protect your nose and mouth from dust. Signal by tapping, whistling or texting if possible. Avoid unnecessary movement.
