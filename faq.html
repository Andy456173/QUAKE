01 / BEFORE

BEFORE AN EARTHQUAKE.

06 QUESTIONS
01What can I do to make my home safer before an earthquake?

Secure tall furniture, shelves, televisions, and other heavy items so they cannot easily fall. Keep heavy or breakable objects on low shelves, move beds away from windows, and keep exits clear. Ask a qualified professional to assess any serious building safety concerns.

02What should I put in an earthquake emergency kit?

Pack drinking water, non-perishable food, essential medicines, basic first-aid supplies, a flashlight, spare batteries, a whistle, sturdy shoes, a power bank, and copies of important documents. Include supplies for children, older relatives, or anyone with specific health needs. Check the kit regularly.

03How can students and schools prepare for earthquakes?

Schools can practise Drop, Cover, and Hold On drills, identify safe places in classrooms, secure heavy furniture, and maintain clear evacuation routes for use after shaking stops. Teachers and students should agree on assembly points and make plans that include people who need assistance.

04Why is a family emergency communication plan important?

Family members may be in different locations when an earthquake occurs. Agree on a safe meeting place, a backup contact person, and ways to communicate if mobile networks are disrupted. Make sure everyone, including children, knows the plan and has important contact details.

05Where should I identify safe places in my home or classroom?

Identify sturdy desks or tables where you can shelter and protect your head and neck. Look for places away from windows, tall bookcases, and objects that could fall. Do not rely on doorways as the safest place.

06Can buildings be made safer before an earthquake?

Yes. Proper building design, maintenance, inspections, and suitable strengthening measures can reduce earthquake damage. Structural changes should be assessed and carried out by qualified engineers or construction professionals rather than attempted as unsafe do-it-yourself repairs.

02 / DURING

DURING AN EARTHQUAKE.

05 QUESTIONS
07What does “Drop, Cover, and Hold On” mean?

Drop onto your hands and knees. Cover your head and neck, preferably under a sturdy table or desk. Hold On to your shelter until the shaking stops. If there is no nearby sturdy shelter, stay low and protect your head and neck with your arms.

08Should I run outside when the ground starts shaking?

No. If you are indoors, stay inside during shaking and use Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Running toward exits increases your risk of being struck by falling glass, debris, or building materials. When shaking stops, assess whether it is safe to evacuate.

09What should I do if I am outdoors or in a vehicle?

If outdoors, stay outside and move to an open area away from buildings, trees, and power lines when possible. If driving, pull over safely away from bridges and overhead hazards, stop, and remain inside the vehicle until the shaking stops.

10What if an earthquake happens while I am asleep?

If you are already in bed, stay there and protect your head and neck with a pillow. Avoid rushing across a room where glass or objects may be falling. When the shaking ends, check for hazards before moving.

11How should someone using a wheelchair respond during shaking?

If you use a wheelchair, lock the wheels if possible, remain seated, and protect your head and neck with your arms, a book, or a cushion. Move away from windows or other hazards only when it is safe and practical to do so.

03 / AFTER

AFTER AN EARTHQUAKE.

04 QUESTIONS
12What should I do immediately after the shaking stops?

Check yourself and others for injuries, put on sturdy shoes if available, and look out for broken glass or unstable objects. Leave a damaged building carefully if it is safe to do so, and follow instructions from local emergency authorities. Do not enter buildings that may be unsafe.

13What are aftershocks, and are they dangerous?

Aftershocks are smaller earthquakes that follow a larger earthquake in the same region. They can occur for days, weeks, or longer and may cause additional damage to weakened buildings. Be ready to Drop, Cover, and Hold On again if shaking returns.

14How can I help other people safely after an earthquake?

Check on nearby people if it is safe, provide basic first aid only within your training, and inform local emergency responders about serious injuries or hazards. Do not enter unstable structures or move severely injured people unless they face immediate danger.

15What if a strong earthquake happens near the coast?

A strong or long earthquake near the coast can be a natural warning of a tsunami. Once shaking stops, move promptly to higher ground or inland, following local evacuation routes and official instructions. Do not wait at the shore to see a wave.

04 / KNOW THE RISK

EARTHQUAKE FACTS.

05 QUESTIONS
16Can scientists predict exactly when an earthquake will happen?

No. Scientists cannot reliably predict the exact time, place, and magnitude of an earthquake. They can identify seismic hazards and estimate longer-term probabilities. This is why preparation before an earthquake is so important.

17Why is Myanmar vulnerable to earthquakes?

Myanmar lies in an active tectonic region and is crossed by significant faults, including the Sagaing Fault. Earthquake damage also depends on local ground conditions, building quality, population exposure, and preparedness.

18What is earthquake disaster risk reduction?

Earthquake disaster risk reduction means taking steps to lower potential harm before a disaster occurs. Examples include improving building safety, securing furniture, organising emergency supplies, practising drills, and creating family or school response plans.

19What is the difference between magnitude and intensity?

Magnitude describes the size of an earthquake at its source. Intensity describes how strongly shaking is felt and what effects occur at a particular place. The same earthquake can have different intensities in different communities.

20Is standing in a doorway safer during an earthquake?

Usually not. A doorway is not generally safer than the rest of a modern building and may expose you to swinging doors or falling objects. Drop, Cover, and Hold On beneath sturdy furniture whenever possible.
